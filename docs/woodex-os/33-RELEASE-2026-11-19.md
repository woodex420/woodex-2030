# WOODEX OS — release report, 19 November 2026, third merge

The report of round 1 of the plan: the shop reads products, categories and series from the Laravel admin's
`/api/v1`, behind a switch of the shop that stays off (T-105). Ten points, in the order of document 10 §9, written
before the merge (document 16 §8).

**On whose word.** The owner's message of 19 November: *"start as you recomend"* — DL-037 for round 3 of the last
merge, and this round's place in document 21 §2 as the first of the eighteen lines that need nobody. The four
questions asked before the first line was written, and his four answers (all as recommended):

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Who prices a basket the shop filled from Laravel? | **Today's rule stands.** The shop *displays* Laravel's products; a basket is still priced by the sales catalogue by the shop's own product number. A price on screen and a price on the order cannot disagree, because only one of them is charged. The disagreement is recorded as a line to close (D-047) |
| How far does the reader go? | **The product list and one product.** Categories, series and pictures already read from Laravel through two bridges that are on; they are untouched |
| How is a published product proved in a browser? | **One product published on the throwaway demo only.** Its database is rebuilt at every start and thrown away; no real product is released |
| How much in this session? | **This round only.** Built, tested, reported, one pull request — then stop and show it |

## 4. Browser/device coverage

The admin itself has no new screen this round: the switch is the shop's, and the admin's screens are unchanged. What
a browser checks is the **shop reading the admin**, which is the point of the round:

| Step | Result |
|---|---|
| The demo workspace starts; a product is published in the admin by a browser, as an editor publishes one | Published. It is the only published product of the demo; the other 170 stay drafts |
| The shop's bridge, pointed at that admin, hands the product to the shop's reader | The product arrives with its name, its price in rupees and its picture |
| Withdrawn in the admin | Gone from the shop's page, without a rebuild |
| The shop's switch off (the shipping default) | The shop reads exactly what it reads today; the address is never reached |

Beyond the four steps above, and only because the first rehearsal **failed**: the product's photograph was a grey box
and the browser was told `401`. See D-048 in §5. Fixed, and then re-shot: the photograph is the uploaded file
(`naturalWidth` 600 × 600, no failed picture request).

Chromium, 1440 × 1000 and 390 × 844. Screenshots: `docs/woodex-os/evidence/native-product-2026-11-19-*.jpg`.

## 5. Defects open/closed by severity

| Severity | Open | Accepted | Closed |
|---|---|---|---|
| Critical | 1 | 0 | 1 |
| High | 0 | 0 | 6 |
| Medium | 7 | 0 | 24 |
| Low | 6 | 0 | 4 |

(D-047 and D-049 are the two new low ones; D-048 and D-050 are the two new mediums, both closed. The numbers are
document 16's own: 50 defects, 36 closed, 1 critical among the open — D-002.)

**Opened and closed this round: D-048.** Found by a browser and by nothing else, after every test in the round was
green — the reader's test builds no page, so no test asked for a photograph. The admin names a product's picture as
a path on **itself** (`/api/v1/catalogue/products/{slug}/picture/{media}/{version}`); a page asks the **shop's**
origin; the shop had a bridge for the categories' pictures and none for the products', and the public allow-list
did not name it either. In the rehearsal the page rendered fully — real name, real breadcrumb from Laravel, "Price on
request", the SKU — with a grey box where the photograph belonged and a `401` the page never looks at. The same
round closed it: a route, the allow-list entry, a test that fails without the route, and the browser shown the
photograph. It is the second time this exact trap has bitten (the categories' bridge carries the first, in a comment
at the allow-list), which is why the entry is written as a paragraph rather than a line.

**Opened and closed: D-050.** The third fault of the round, and the first that no test could have found because the
test was written from the same wrong assumption as the code. The reader gave each option of a product a `id` that
was its position in the list. The shop keeps the option a customer chose in a basket and in a quote as `variantId`
and matches a saved line back to an option by it (`contexts/reconcile.ts`). The admin has no `id` of its own to give
an option — the contract gives none — so reordering a product's options in the editor would have silently pointed a
saved chair at a different chair, with a plausible price and nothing on screen. The id is now derived from the SKU,
which the contract does make unique, so the same option keeps the same number across reorders, republications and
neighbours being removed. The test fails if the position comes back.

**Opened: D-049 (low)** — a second fault the browser found and this report does not fix: on a published product's
page the option pill says "Price on request" while the product-level line is right. It is cosmetic, it is not in
this round's two acceptance criteria (a product list and a product page that render what Laravel published), and it
belongs to the sales-catalogue price rule of D-047. Left open, named here.

**Closed this round: D-048 and D-050.** No other defect was in reach of the round. **Opened: D-047 (low)** — a basket the
shop fills from Laravel would be *priced* by the sales catalogue while *displaying* Laravel's price, which the owner
chose deliberately for this round and which is therefore a line to close rather than a fault: it stands open with
T-105's follow-up, so that the two cannot be forgotten together.

The one open critical defect is unchanged and is the owner's: **D-002**, the old dashboard's owner password in a
public document (T-021). Gate E cannot pass while it is open.

## 10. Explicit owner approval status

**Nothing is accepted and nothing is switched on.** The shop's switch is **off**: `NATIVE_PRODUCT_SYNC` is
unset, and with it unset the Node API's `/api/products` answers from the shop's own database exactly as it does
today. Nothing typed or seen on the live shop changes by this merge.

What the owner is asked, and only this: **whether to switch the shop's reader on**, which is one setting on the
shop's Vercel project (`NATIVE_PRODUCT_SYNC=1`, beside the `NATIVE_CATALOGUE_ORIGIN` that points at the admin).
It is not proposed today, because it would show a shop of **nothing but drafts**: all 170 real products are
drafts, and a shop reading Laravel's published products would have none. The order is therefore: publish the
products (T-090's workflow, already built), then switch the reader on.

## What this round did not do

- **It changed no price rule.** The basket is still priced by the sales catalogue by the shop's own product
  number; what the shop shows now comes from Laravel, and what it charges does not. That is D-047, open, and the
  owner's answer for this round.
- **It published nothing real.** The one product published is on the demo, whose database is thrown away. The 170
  real products stay drafts, and publishing one is the owner's act through the admin's own review.
- **It added no index.** The products list orders by change time and filters on a JSON column; with 170 products
  the cost is invisible, and the moment the catalogue is hundreds of products this is the first thing to measure.
- **It did not touch the categories, series or pictures** that already read from Laravel: two bridges on, working,
  and unchanged.
- **It closed two defects and opened two**, all named in §5 — and both of the ones it closed were faults in this
  round's own new code, found after every test in the round was green. D-048 was found by a browser, which is the
  argument for the browser step being in the report at all. D-050 was found by reading the reader against the shop's
  own basket code, and no test could have found it, because the test was written from the same wrong assumption as
  the code: an id that was a position. Both are written as the round's own lesson rather than a footnote. D-047 records the price decision rather than leaving it in a conversation; D-049 is
  a cosmetic pill left open on purpose.
